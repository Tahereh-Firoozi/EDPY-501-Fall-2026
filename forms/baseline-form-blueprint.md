# Research Thinking and AI Use Baseline

## Form introduction

This activity helps the instructor understand the range of experiences, assumptions, and learning needs students bring to EDPY 501. There are no “correct” research perspectives in Part A. Respond based on what you currently think rather than what you believe a research-methods instructor may expect. The activity is assessed for completion, not correctness.

**Recommended setting:** Do not display automatic correctness scores. If completion credit requires identification, collect only the minimum course identifier permitted by the approved platform and course privacy requirements.

## Part A — How do you think about research?

**Response scale for all 12 items:**

1 — Strongly disagree  
2 — Disagree  
3 — Neither agree nor disagree  
4 — Agree  
5 — Strongly agree

1. There is usually one best research method for answering an educational research question.
2. The strongest research is research in which the researcher has as little influence as possible on the findings.
3. The context in which an educational phenomenon occurs can change how it should be understood.
4. Participants’ interpretations of their own experiences can be important forms of research evidence.
5. Numerical evidence is generally more trustworthy than narrative evidence.
6. Researchers’ experiences and values can influence research decisions even when rigorous procedures are followed.
7. An important feature of strong research is that another researcher could obtain similar findings under similar conditions.
8. Different individuals can experience the same educational situation differently, and these different interpretations may all be meaningful.
9. The research question should determine the methods used rather than the researcher’s preference for a particular method.
10. Research can sometimes have the explicit purpose of addressing inequities or bringing about social or educational change.
11. When researchers have personal experience with the topic they are studying, they should reflect on and make that relationship visible.
12. What counts as useful evidence depends partly on the kind of research question being asked.

## Part B — Research decision scenarios

### Scenario 1

A university discovers that attendance at optional research-methods workshops has declined substantially. The university wants to understand why.

**What would you investigate first?**

- Analyze attendance patterns and student characteristics.
- Interview students about why they attend or do not attend.
- Analyze attendance patterns first and then interview selected students to explain the patterns.
- Invite students to collaboratively investigate the problem and redesign the workshops.
- Something else.

**Follow-up, open response:** Briefly explain why you selected this approach.

### Scenario 2 — Open response

Two researchers study the same classroom and reach somewhat different conclusions. Does this necessarily mean that one of them is wrong? Explain your reasoning.

### Scenario 3 — Open response

Complete this sentence in your own words: **I consider research evidence trustworthy when…**

### Scenario 4 — Open response

Imagine that you were conducting research on an educational issue that you have personally experienced. How, if at all, might your own experience affect the study?

## Part D — AI literacy confidence

**Response scale:** 1 — Not confident to 5 — Very confident

1. I can explain how a generative AI system differs from an academic database or search engine.
2. I know that an AI-generated reference may look authentic even when the source does not exist.
3. I can verify factual claims made by an AI system.
4. I can determine whether scholarly sources recommended by an AI tool actually support its claims.
5. I understand potential privacy concerns when entering information into an AI tool.
6. I can identify situations in which AI should not be used for research.
7. I can construct an effective prompt by specifying context, task, constraints, and expected output.
8. I understand that AI output can reproduce biases contained in training data or prompts.
9. I know when AI use should be disclosed in academic work.
10. I understand that I remain responsible for academic work even when AI contributed to it.

## Instructor interpretation notes — do not place in the student form

Treat responses as patterns rather than scores or fixed labels.

| Orientation or awareness | Items offering clues |
|---|---|
| Postpositivist or empirical | 2, 5, 7, and partly 1 |
| Constructivist or interpretive | 3, 4, 8, and 11 |
| Pragmatic | 9 and 12 |
| Transformative or critical | 10 |
| Reflexive awareness | 6 and 11 |

Students may reasonably draw on several orientations. Avoid labelling an individual student from this introductory diagnostic.
