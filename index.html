Activity 1 · Small groups · 15 minutes

What counts as research?

For each case, choose Research, Not yet research, or It depends. Your group must be able to defend the reasoning behind each choice.

Case 1

A professor notices that students who sit near the front receive higher grades.

Discussion: This is an observation that could motivate research. It becomes research when the question, evidence, procedures, analysis, and limits of any claim are made systematic and transparent.

Case 2

A researcher interviews 20 first-generation students and systematically analyzes recurring meanings.

Discussion: This can be qualitative research if sampling, consent, interviewing, analysis, researcher positioning, and interpretation are handled rigorously and transparently.

Case 3

An instructor asks a generative AI tool why students are disengaged and reports its answer to colleagues.

Discussion: The AI response may generate hypotheses, but it is not empirical evidence about these students. The claims still need an appropriate study and credible sources.

Case 4

Researchers administer a validated belonging scale to 500 students and examine predictors of belonging.

Discussion: This can be quantitative research. The quality of the evidence still depends on the design, sampling, measurement, analysis, and whether the conclusions stay within what the study can support.

Case 5

A faculty member compares grades before and after changing a course assessment.

Discussion: It depends on the purpose and procedures. A before–after comparison can be systematic inquiry, but the absence of a comparison condition and other changes may limit causal conclusions.

Case 6

A graduate student reads 12 studies about feedback and writes a transparent, critical synthesis.

Discussion: A review can be research when its question, search and selection decisions, appraisal, synthesis, and limitations are transparent. Simply summarizing convenient sources is not enough.
A method does not make a study rigorous. Rigor comes from the relationship among the question, evidence, method, analysis, and claim.

AI literacy check · Pairs · 8 minutes

A convincing answer is not yet trustworthy evidence

An AI tool gives you five recent “peer-reviewed” studies, complete with DOIs and concise summaries, to support the claim that AI improves university learning.

Before using the material in a proposal, what would you do?

  1. LocateFind each source in a credible database, library catalogue, or publisher site.
  2. ConfirmVerify that the article, authors, journal details, year, and DOI actually exist.
  3. ReadConsult the original source rather than relying on the AI-generated summary.
  4. CheckDetermine whether the study really supports the claim and whether its design is relevant.
  5. EvaluateAssess the study’s quality, limitations, context, and applicability.
  6. CiteCite the original scholarship and disclose AI use when course or disciplinary guidance requires it.
AI can assist research. It cannot assume scholarly responsibility.
