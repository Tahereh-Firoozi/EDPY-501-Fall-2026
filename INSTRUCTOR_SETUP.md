## Recommended response-identity approach

The baseline is intended to elicit honest assumptions rather than “correct-looking” answers. If students receive completion credit, collect only one minimal course identifier through the approved platform. If candid anonymous responses are more important, separate the completion check from the diagnostic responses. Apply University and course privacy requirements to whichever option you choose.

## Activity 1 facilitation

Ask groups to classify the six cases before revealing the debrief prompts. The objective is not a simple score. Press students to explain what additional information would be needed, what claim could be supported, and what limitations remain.

Central debrief:

> A method does not make a study rigorous. Rigor comes from the relationship among the question, evidence, method, analysis, and claim.

## Activity 2 facilitation

Assign groups to quantitative, qualitative, or mixed-methods approaches so all three are represented. Ask each group to prepare a two-minute explanation of:

1. its purpose and research question;
2. the evidence needed;
3. how the evidence would be analyzed; and
4. one conclusion the proposed design would not justify.

When comparing groups, emphasize that the approaches allow researchers to ask different questions and construct different kinds of knowledge. For mixed methods, ask exactly where and why integration occurs.

## AI scenario facilitation

Give pairs time to draft their own verification plan before revealing the six-step workflow. Ask which steps are source verification, which are methodological appraisal, and which involve academic responsibility.

Central debrief:

> AI can assist research. It cannot assume scholarly responsibility.

## End-of-term reuse

Repeat selected baseline items and the same prompt-writing task in Session 13. Students can compare their September and December thinking without treating research worldviews as fixed personality types.
