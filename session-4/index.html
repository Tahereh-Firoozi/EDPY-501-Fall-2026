PAGE 2 / RESEARCH PROPOSAL

Classroom Participation and Sense of Belonging Among First-Year International and Indigenous University Students

An Explanatory Sequential Mixed Methods Study

IntroductionGapTheoryQuestionsMethodsData

01

Introduction

Classroom participation can provide students with opportunities to test ideas, seek clarification, interact with peers, and develop relationships with instructors. Yet participation is not experienced in the same way by all students, particularly during the first year of university when students are learning new academic and social expectations.

International students may be adjusting to unfamiliar instructional practices and expectations for classroom interaction. Indigenous students may encounter university environments in which their identities, experiences, or ways of participating are not consistently reflected in dominant classroom practices. These students may therefore experience lower confidence and greater difficulty adapting to conventional expectations for visible classroom participation.

02

Research gap

Previous research has linked student engagement, classroom participation, and sense of belonging. However, many studies rely on self-reported participation rather than observing participation as it occurs. Quantitative studies can identify patterns but may not explain why students participate differently under particular classroom conditions. International and Indigenous students may also be included within larger samples without sufficient attention to how classroom relationships and perceptions of inclusion shape their experiences.

The proposed study therefore integrates reported sense of belonging + observed classroom participation + students’ explanations of participation decisions.

03

Theoretical framework

The study is guided by Strayhorn’s Student Sense of Belonging framework. Sense of belonging concerns whether students perceive themselves as accepted, valued, included, and connected within an educational environment. The framework is especially relevant during transitions and for students whose identities or experiences may differ from dominant institutional norms.

Participation is therefore treated not simply as an individual characteristic, but as behaviour that may be shaped by acceptance, relationships with instructors and peers, classroom norms, and whether students feel that their contributions are valued.

04

Significance and purpose

Instructors often interpret visible behaviours such as asking questions or speaking during discussion as indicators of engagement. The same behaviour, however, may have different meanings for different students. Combining observation, survey data, and student accounts may provide a more nuanced understanding of participation and help explain why international and Indigenous students sometimes struggle to participate successfully in university classrooms.

Purpose: To examine the relationship between classroom participation and sense of belonging among first-year undergraduate students, with particular attention to international and Indigenous students, and to use interviews to explain quantitative patterns.

05

Research questions

  1. What patterns of classroom participation are observed among first-year undergraduate students during whole-class and small-group activities?
  2. What is the relationship between students’ reported sense of belonging and their observed classroom participation?
  3. How do patterns of classroom participation and sense of belonging vary among international students, Indigenous students, and other domestic students?
  4. How do international and Indigenous students describe the ways that acceptance, classroom relationships, and perceptions of inclusion influence their decisions to participate or not participate?
  5. How do students’ interview accounts help explain patterns identified in the survey and classroom-observation data?

06

Methodology and setting

The study will use an explanatory sequential mixed methods design. Phase 1 will collect quantitative survey and structured classroom-observation data. Phase 2 will use semi-structured interviews to explain selected quantitative patterns. Integration will occur during interpretation through a joint display linking observed behaviour, survey results, and student explanations.

The study will take place in two first-year undergraduate courses at a large Canadian university. Each course enrolls approximately 45–55 students. One course is taught by the principal investigator; the other is taught by another instructor in the same department. The department chair and both instructors have agreed to allow the study to take place in their classrooms.

07

Participants and recruitment

Approximately 100 first-year students will be invited to participate. The researcher expects approximately 15–20 international students, 8–12 Indigenous students, and 65–75 other domestic students.

The study will be introduced during regular class time. Students who participate will sign a form before leaving class. Students in the course taught by the principal investigator will receive 2% toward their final course grade for participating. The researcher will retain the participation list so the credit can be entered before final grades are submitted. International and Indigenous students will indicate their group membership on the sign-up form so that sufficient numbers can later be invited to interviews. Participation will be described as voluntary.

08

Phase 1 · Questionnaire and classroom observation

The 15-minute online questionnaire will collect demographic information, international/domestic status, Indigenous identity, sense-of-belonging scores, comfort with classroom participation, and perceived participation frequency. Students will not enter names; identification numbers will link questionnaire and observation data.

The researcher will observe four sessions in each course and record participation behaviours such as asking questions, answering the instructor, contributing to whole-class or small-group discussion, and requesting help. Audio recordings of the entire class will be made to check participation events. Because the department chair and instructors have approved the study, the full classroom will be observed and recorded. Students who do not complete the questionnaire may still appear in observation notes or recordings.

09

Phase 2 · Interviews

Approximately 10 international students and 10 Indigenous students will be purposively selected using patterns from Phase 1. Interviews will last 45–60 minutes and will explore situations in which students feel comfortable participating, decisions not to speak, peer and instructor influences, classroom expectations, and experiences of inclusion.

Participants will be told that interviews concern classroom participation, university learning, and belonging. If participants raise experiences involving discrimination, financial difficulty, family circumstances, mental-health concerns, or previous trauma, the researcher may ask follow-up questions when those experiences appear relevant. Students will be told that interviews are anonymous and that their names will not appear in publications.

10

Analysis and mixed methods integration

Descriptive statistics will summarize belonging scores and participation behaviours. The researcher will examine the relationship between sense of belonging and observed participation and conduct exploratory comparisons across international, Indigenous, and other domestic students.

Interview transcripts will be analyzed thematically for patterns related to acceptance, instructor practices, peer relationships, cultural expectations, language, and decisions about participation. A joint display will integrate observed behaviour → survey result → student explanation to identify convergence, complementarity, and divergence across the two phases.

11

Data management

Questionnaire data will be stored in a password-protected electronic file. Interview and classroom audio recordings will initially be downloaded to the researcher’s personal password-protected laptop. A graduate research assistant will transcribe interviews; recordings will be sent to the assistant through regular university email. Names will be replaced by participant IDs in transcripts. Files will be retained until analysis and publication are complete; a specific destruction date has not been established.

12

Dissemination

Findings will be reported in conference presentations and a peer-reviewed article. Pseudonyms will replace names. To preserve context, quotations may be accompanied by descriptors such as “Indigenous student, second year, Education program” or “International student from East Asia, first-year Education student.” The Indigenous subgroup is expected to be small, and several participants may come from the same program.
