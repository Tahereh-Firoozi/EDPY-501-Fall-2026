The five jobs of an introduction

Creswell and Creswell call this a guide for building a rationale. A published article may combine these jobs or place parts of the literature review elsewhere; the order is a reading aid, not a required five-paragraph rule.

  1. 1. ProblemWhat educational concern needs attention?
  2. 2. Prior workWhat do existing studies already tell us?
  3. 3. GapWhat specific point remains unclear?
  4. 4. SignificanceWho could use the answer, and why?
  5. 5. PurposeWhat will this study examine?

This is an invented teaching example. Its statements about previous research are for practice, not claims you should cite as evidence.

BPrior studies suggest that structured peer feedback can help students revise their drafts.

CHowever, these studies tell us less about how novice writers decide to accept or set aside particular comments.

DUnderstanding those decisions could help writing instructors teach students how to evaluate feedback.

EThis interview study will explore how first-year undergraduates explain their decisions to use or set aside peer comments after revising one assignment.
